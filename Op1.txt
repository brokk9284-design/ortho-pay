Architecture Principles
The development agent should adhere to these principles throughout the project:
1. API-first: All business operations must be executed through Edge Functions; clients must never interact directly with financial tables.
2. Event-driven: Model long-running workflows (matching, escrow, settlement, notifications) as asynchronous events rather than synchronous request chains.
3. Stateless frontend: Treat the frontend as a presentation layer. Critical state belongs in the backend or cache.
4. Security by default: Enforce Row Level Security, server-side authorization, idempotency, and immutable audit logs.
5. Performance: Cache frequently accessed, non-sensitive data in Redis. Avoid repetitive database polling.
6. Modularity: Keep business domains (matching, escrow, reputation, fees, compliance) isolated so they can evolve independently.
7. Observability: Every critical action must be logged, traceable, and monitored with alerts for failures.
8. Scalability: Design today so services like the Matching Engine or Fraud Engine can later be extracted without major rewrites.
9. Infrastructure simplicity: Prefer managed serverless services (Supabase, Vercel, Upstash) until scale clearly justifies additional operational complexity.
10. Business logic over CRUD: The database stores state; Edge Functions implement financial rules. Never let database structure dictate business workflows.

Business Rules
The following rules apply throughout the platform:
1. The platform is a marketplace, not the default counterparty.
2. Every financial action must pass through escrow or an equivalent trust mechanism.
3. Trust and liquidity scores directly influence marketplace outcomes.
4. Fees are dynamic and transparent before confirmation.
5. Matching prioritises overall quality, not simply arrival order.
6. Partial fulfilment is supported where practical.
7. Every financial action is fully auditable.
8. Every settlement updates reputation, liquidity metrics, and marketplace analytics.
9. External payment rails are abstracted behind a unified settlement experience.
10. The system should continuously optimise matching, routing, and pricing using historical performance data while maintaining fairness and transparency.
---
Vision Statement
ORTHO-PAY should evolve beyond a simple peer-to-peer payment application into a global liquidity marketplace that connects consumers, businesses, merchants, and liquidity providers through intelligent matching, escrow-backed trust, dynamic pricing, and multi-rail settlement.
The long-term objective is to become the trusted orchestration layer for peer-to-peer and business payments across the UK, Europe, the United States, Canada, and future markets, where reputation, reliability, and efficient liquidity allocation create a strong and defensible network effect.
